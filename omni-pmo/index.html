EngineBackend

What runs before a screen exists

Apps Script · V8 No server, no bundler
1
Intake

Vendors email daily production reports as spreadsheets in whatever shape they use. A Power Automate flow drops them into a watched Drive folder and a timed trigger discovers new files. Deliberately not a webhook, because a few seconds of latency is worth not owning a receiver.

Drive discovery · timed trigger
2
Schema mapping

Every vendor names columns differently and changes them without warning. One adapter resolves headers by alias rather than position, so a shifted or renamed column degrades to a warning instead of silently writing footage into the wrong field.

createSchemaAdapter
3
Matching and dedup

Project IDs arrive misspelled, transposed, or with the wrong market prefix. Fuzzy matching resolves the typo and records it as a soft match rather than fixing it invisibly. A dedup guard stops the same report being counted twice when a vendor resends.

Matching engine · dedup guard
4
Archive and mirror

Clean rows append to the master archive, the single history of what was reported. A derived review sheet mirrors current state, so analysis never mutates the record it reads from.

Master archive · daily review
5
Reconciliation

A two-phase asynchronous sync pulls the enterprise system of record and compares it against reported actuals. Disagreements become explicit adjustment records with a reason, not a quiet overwrite. Bill-of-materials quantities reconcile against what crews actually used.

Reconciliation engine · async sync
6
Visibility and diagnostics

One engine decides which projects are eligible for the portfolio and generates a placeholder row for projects that should exist but carry no production history. Diagnostics classify every finding into the flags the surfaces render.

The pace bead, not a second number

Every velocity bar carries a bead at the position this material should occupy today. Behind-schedule is then a spatial gap you see rather than a subtraction you perform, and the bars sit in an inset well so the fill reads as filling something. Four bars, one glance, no arithmetic.

A risk has to name its own cause

A severity band that says high and stops is a number pretending to be an assessment. Every risk here carries three lines: the driver, the action someone has committed to, and the downstream exposure if it slips. A risk nobody can act on is just an alarm, and the structure is what forces the distinction at the moment of writing.

The owned date sits on a plinth that rises from the bottom of the card with an upward shadow, so it reads as a foundation rather than another panel. CaseStudy

Why a project manager built a platform

The operating problem

I managed roughly forty concurrent fiber builds across nine municipalities and four construction vendors. The work generated a daily flood of vendor production reports, and the enterprise system of record held the schedule, but nothing joined them. Determining whether a single project was actually behind meant opening several spreadsheets, reconciling names that did not match, and trusting a status field nobody had updated in a week.

The cost showed up in two places. Preparing for weekly meetings took six to eight hours of manual assembly. The daily review that should have caught problems early was too expensive to do properly, so problems surfaced late, usually as a surprise in front of a director.

What I built

OmniSight is an operating platform for the delivery portfolio: an ingestion and reconciliation engine underneath, four coordinated surfaces on top. It runs on Google Apps Script against Sheets and the enterprise system's API, with no server and no build step, because those were the constraints I actually had.

  • Ingestion: vendor reports are discovered, normalized through an alias-based schema adapter, matched against known projects, deduplicated, and appended to a single archive of record.
  • Reconciliation: a two-phase asynchronous sync compares the enterprise system against reported actuals and writes explicit adjustment records rather than silently overwriting either side.
  • Diagnostics: stale reporting, impossible production pace, inferred state, blocked predecessors, bill-of-materials variance and special crossings are each classified into a flag the interface renders consistently everywhere.
  • Surfaces: the same portfolio read four ways, because a dashboard answers a different question than a timeline, a queue, or a risk map.

The design principle underneath

Every visual device in this product was chosen to move a question out of the reader's working memory, and the design vocabulary above sets out seventeen of them with the specimen next to the argument. Each one also links to the live component it describes: turn on Anatomy in the walkthrough and the argument attaches to the real thing. The principle they share is worth stating on its own.

A delivery portfolio is not read, it is scanned, usually a few minutes before someone has to answer for it in a meeting. Under that condition, anything the reader has to decode, remember across a gap, or cross-reference is a cost. So state gets encoded in form as well as number — a stripe on an edge, a texture in a fill, a letter in a bead, a glow on the frame — because form resolves in peripheral vision and number does not. And the encoding has to be the same everywhere, or the reader is learning a new language on each surface.

The corollary is that nothing is decorative. Every colour, radius, weight and border on a surface is either carrying meaning or is a mistake. That constraint is what makes the density survivable: at forty projects, a screen with one ornamental element is a screen with one more thing to filter out.

The second principle is that findings are surfaced, never silently corrected. A resolved typo shows as a soft match, a derived stage shows as inferred, a reconciliation writes an adjustment record with a reason. An automation that quietly fixes things teaches its operator to stop looking, and an operator who has stopped looking is worse than no automation at all.

When a later rebuild replaced these devices with generic cards and a plain risk band, the density collapsed and the surface stopped answering questions at a glance. Being able to say precisely why is the part of this work I would most want to be judged on.

My role, stated precisely

I was the Construction Project Manager, not a software engineer on a platform team. I conceived the system, specified it, designed the interface and the data model, tested it, and operated it daily while running the portfolio it served. The code was written with AI assistance under my direction. I am the product owner, systems designer, domain expert, tester and operator; I did not hand-write the entire codebase and do not claim to have.

It was a self-initiated tool, not an assigned project and not a company product.
