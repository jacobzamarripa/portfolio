Four years in the field,
four tools that shipped.

I installed and repaired fiber for four years and built tools the entire time, starting with spreadsheet formulas as a technician in 2022 and ending with a project tracker I demonstrated to the VP in 2025. Four of them were used for real work. Nexus, the application below, is the retrospective rebuild of that body of work into one role-aware system, and it is the only thing here that was never deployed.

02 / Case study

From field friction to system design

The operating problem

Fiber work crosses several handoffs: scheduling, customer readiness, installation, splicing, provisioning, QA, transfer claims, and closeout. When those steps live in separate trackers, the technician reconstructs the day while dispatch and leadership reconstruct the portfolio. The expensive failure wasn't any single step — it was re-keying the same record into each one.

What I actually built, and where

I started in 2022 with a barcode app that was entirely spreadsheet formulas. The technician pasted the assigned work into the sheet, formulas corrected what they could, and the sheet generated groups of scannable codes, one group per system, each carrying the address, customer, serial and ONT ID together. The technician scanned the matching group into every system that held the record, so all of them ended up saying the same thing. No integration, no API, just a common symbol that systems never designed to talk to each other could all read. That pipeline is the part that remained in use after I left.

On promotion to Fiber Installation Lead I built L-FIT, the Lead Fiber Installation Tech Tracker, a name my partner and I made up. It held what the official schedule didn't, and it did the real data cleansing the barcode sheet never could. It recorded what was completed and when, which is what made a technician-output dashboard possible. I sent that dashboard to my manager and the VP every month.

At the end, a third. In December 2025 I built a project tracker for the Austin and San Antonio deployments: an eight-phase milestone model with slippage tracking, fuzzy matching so one community couldn't quietly become two, and an executive dashboard grouped by market. I demonstrated it to the VP and upper management, one team ran on it, and I wrote the adoption guide for the manager who would keep it after I left.

The point was never the codes. It was that the association between an address and the equipment installed at it stopped depending on somebody typing fourteen characters correctly at the end of a long day.

Why it stuck: keying vs scanning

Every closeout needed the ONT ID typed by hand against the address. One transposed character meant a failed association and, often, a second truck roll. Try typing this one exactly.

Nexus is the retrospective part. After I left, I rebuilt both tools as one role-aware application — the walkthrough above. It was never deployed at Centric and I claim no production adoption for it. The QR pipeline is the piece that was real, and it remained in use after my departure.

What I designed into Nexus

  • Technician dayflow: first stop, current work, future queue, claims, provisioning, and field guides.
  • Operational control: dispatch board, calendar, map context, intake, status lifecycle, and end-of-day reporting.
  • Quality layer: structured splicing and equipment fields, QA exceptions, and performance views.
  • Role boundaries: technician, lead, dispatcher, manager, and administrator views expose only the work each role needs.

What I scoped but never shipped

An in-app SOP library anyone could reference, and an iPad rollout the whole interface was being optimized for. Both were real plans. Neither was built, and I don't count them as delivery.

My role and the boundary

My formal Centric roles were In-Home Installation Technician (October 2022–December 2024) and Fiber Installation Lead (January 2025–January 2026). I performed fusion splicing, OTDR validation, troubleshooting, quality checks, and employee training. I did not hold a Project Manager title there, and nothing on this page should be read as claiming one.
