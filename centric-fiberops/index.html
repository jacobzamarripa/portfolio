02 / Case study

From field friction to system design

The operating problem

Fiber work crosses several handoffs: scheduling, customer readiness, installation, splicing, provisioning, QA, transfer claims, and closeout. When those steps live in separate trackers, the technician reconstructs the day while dispatch and leadership reconstruct the portfolio. The expensive failure wasn't any single step — it was re-keying the same record into each one.

What I actually built, and where

Two tools, during the job. L-FIT — the Lead Fiber Installation Tech Tracker, a name my partner and I made up — held what the official schedule didn't. Alongside it I built a QR pipeline: pull the assigned work from the system of record through its API, cleanse it, generate a scannable code carrying the address, customer, serial and ONT ID together, scan that into the provisioning platform, then write back so both systems agreed. It recorded what was completed and when, which is what made a real technician-output dashboard possible for managers.

The point was never the codes. It was that the association between an address and the equipment installed at it stopped depending on somebody typing fourteen characters correctly at the end of a long day.

Nexus is the retrospective part. After I left, I rebuilt both tools as one role-aware application — the walkthrough above. It was never deployed at Centric and I claim no production adoption for it. The QR pipeline is the piece that was real, and it remained in use after my departure.

What I designed into Nexus

  • Technician dayflow: first stop, current work, future queue, claims, provisioning, and field guides.
  • Operational control: dispatch board, calendar, map context, intake, status lifecycle, and end-of-day reporting.
  • Quality layer: structured splicing and equipment fields, QA exceptions, and performance views.
  • Role boundaries: technician, lead, dispatcher, manager, and administrator views expose only the work each role needs.

What I scoped but never shipped

An in-app SOP library anyone could reference, and an iPad rollout the whole interface was being optimized for. Both were real plans. Neither was built, and I don't count them as delivery.

My role and the boundary

My formal Centric roles were In-Home Installation Technician (October 2022–December 2024) and Fiber Installation Lead (January 2025–January 2026). I performed fusion splicing, OTDR validation, troubleshooting, quality checks, and employee training. I did not hold a Project Manager title there, and nothing on this page should be read as claiming one.
