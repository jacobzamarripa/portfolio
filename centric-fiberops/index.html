Nexus FiberOps Unified

Four field tools, rebuilt as one app

From October 2022 to January 2026 I installed fiber at Centric Fiber, first as a technician, then as an installation lead. Along the way I built four tools that made the technicians’ work easier, and all four were used for real work. After I left, I rebuilt them as one field app to see how they would work designed together: built for the iPad, mobile-first throughout. Nexus was never deployed, and the records inside are invented.

Fourteen characters at the end of a long day

Every install ended at a keyboard. The technician typed the ONT ID, the serial and the address into each system that needed them. One transposed character meant a failed association, and often a second truck roll. Recalled

That cost never showed up upstream. It landed on the technician, after the work was done. So in 2022, still a technician myself, I started replacing the typing with scanning.

Four tools, in the order I built them

I never held a project manager title at Centric. Each tool started with a problem the technicians hit every day, and each was used for real work. The colours are the first four in the fiber code every installer knows.

  1. 1

    2022

    QR install workflow

    Built entirely from spreadsheet formulas. The technician pastes the assignment into the sheet, the formulas clean up what they can, and the sheet generates groups of scannable codes for the address, customer, serial and ONT, one group per system. The technician scans each group into its system, and every system ends up saying the same thing.

    The goal was for every system to agree. New-build addresses weren’t in any public map yet, so what mattered was every system calling the same house by the same name. Recalled

    Technicians who still use it timed the closeout at about 2 minutes, down from about 10. Recalled

  2. 2

    2025

    L-FIT tracker

    After I became Fiber Installation Lead, I built L-FIT to record what was actually installed and when, which the official schedule didn’t show. That record made a monthly dashboard of technician output possible, and I sent it to my manager and the VP every month.

    That dashboard is what led a former director to offer me my first project manager role, in November 2025.

Designed as one

Rebuilding the tools together showed what each one had been missing on its own, and what a real rollout would still have to answer.

Decisions in the prototype

  • Today firstThe technician opens on the first stop and the next action. No portfolio rollup, no percentages.
  • One record, everywhereSelect a job on the map, the calendar or the list, and it is selected everywhere.
  • Validate before the codeA bad code that scans cleanly puts wrong data into provisioning with full confidence. A record that fails its check never gets one.
  • Beside dispatchDispatchers keep the tools they already use. Nexus owns the technician’s day and the manager’s view of it.

What I would settle before a real rollout

  • OfflineTechnicians work where there is no signal. The prototype assumes a connection.
  • The deviceGloves, glare and battery life on a tablet that lives in a truck.
  • The data contractThe pipeline works only while the assignment export keeps its shape. Someone has to own that shape, or it breaks quietly the first time a column moves.
  • Field guidesThe SOP library was planned and never built.

My part

My titles at Centric were In-Home Installation Technician, then Fiber Installation Lead. Day to day that meant installs, fusion splicing, OTDR testing, troubleshooting and quality checks, and training the technicians who came after me.

The tools came from the same place: a problem I hit on the job, then a fix the next person could use without me. The 2022 workflow is formulas I wrote in a spreadsheet. By 2025, AI was turning ideas like these into applications. For Nexus, I wrote the specs, directed the AI-assisted build, tested every screen and decided what went in.
