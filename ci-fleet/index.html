Markup

Replace the vans with what?

By the time of the challenge, Centric had already decided to replace its service vans. The open question was what should replace them.

So the proposal had one job: describe the right vehicle precisely enough for Fleet Operations to evaluate it. A standing-height cargo van is built to haul volume. The service technicians driving them made five or more stops a day, and at every stop they climbed into the cargo bay to find a tool and climbed back out. Recorded

Service techs are tool-dense, not volume-dense. They need less space with more structure.

Markup

Less space, more structure

From the loadout I laid out four storage zones and specified the parts to build them.

Everything that has to stay dry and locked rides under the cap. Tools live in one full-width drawer that slides out at the tailgate. Long items ride in a locking tube on the rack, mounted so the tailgate still opens. Recorded

Would it carry the load?

Before recommending the truck I checked its payload. Against a 1,500 lb rating, the upfit came to 458 lb, the heavy equipment to 214 lb and tools and materials to about 250 to 350 lb. That left a margin of 478 to 578 lb. Recorded

Review

Whose decision it was

I worked it the way a consultant would: an outside view, a costed recommendation, and the decision left with the people who own it. The proposal closed with a three-phase plan and one sentence about who decides. Recalled

This roadmap is a proposed starting point for Fleet Management to evaluate and adapt as needed. Final execution timing, vendor selection, and rollout strategy would be determined by Fleet Operations.

From the proposal Recorded
  1. Phase 1Pilot validation

    One truck built to the configuration and driven by an active technician for 30 to 60 days, with feedback written down and adjustments noted.

  2. Phase 2Vendors and procurement

    Fleet Management finalizes vendors, sources the base vehicle through its existing fleet channels and sets up the installer process.

  3. Phase 3Rollout

    Phased, as vans reach their normal replacement cycle, with technician onboarding run by Fleet Operations.

RevChangeWhy
ACheck every number against the thing it describesThe proposal said long items ran 8 to 12 ft, in six places. They were about 5 ft, which the parts list already specified. The requirement was the error.
BPrice the whole truckI priced the upfit and left out total cost of ownership and payback, the first things a fleet budget asks about.
CPut the people in the payloadThe driver and any rider count against the rating. The margin was smaller than the table showed.
DMeasure the time savedFive to ten minutes a day was my estimate, with no time study behind it.
EAnswer the workspace questionA standing-height van is also a dry place to splice in rain and heat. The proposal didn’t say how a truck handles that.

In service

In service

Technicians are driving the trucks now. They were built with the main components of the specification: the enclosed cap, the drawer system and the tool storage. Some details are being adjusted by position, so the setup varies with the role. Recalled

The recommendation was adopted. What the trucks have changed in the field isn’t measured yet, so there are no fuel, time or cost results here. The next step is to measure one truck against the old van baseline: time from parking to the first tool in hand, warehouse trips per week, fuel per mile, and where splicing happens when the weather turns.
